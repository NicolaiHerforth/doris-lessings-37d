## Executive Summary

The overall legal risk of this setup is **LOW to MEDIUM**, primarily because all inquiries route through the mægler (no private sale attempt) and the website functions as supplementary promotion. The main risks are: (1) using the mægler's personal details without consent, (2) potential liability for incorrect property information, and (3) missing mandatory disclosures in advertising. ## 1. Formidlingsaftale Compliance

**Risk Level: LOW**

### The Law

The formidlingsaftale (governed by Ejendomsmæglerloven / Lov om formidling af fast ejendom m.v.) is an agreement granting the mægler the right to *formidle* (mediate/broker) the sale. It does **not** prohibit the owner from promoting their own property. The seller remains the legal owner and party to the sale — the mægler is an intermediary.

### Key Points

- **No exclusivity on marketing.** The formidlingsaftale typically specifies what marketing the *mægler* will do (and what the seller pays for). It does not generally contain a clause forbidding the seller from doing supplementary marketing.
- **No "private sale" conflict.** Since all inquiries are routed to the mægler, you are not circumventing the formidlingsaftale. You're generating leads *for* the mægler.
- **Potential contractual nuance.** Some formidlingsaftaler may contain clauses about the seller not interfering with or undermining the mægler's efforts. Supplementary promotion that drives traffic *to* the mægler is the opposite of undermining.

### Recommendation

- **Read your formidlingsaftale carefully** for any clause restricting seller-initiated marketing or requiring mægler approval for promotional materials.
- If no such clause exists (most standard agreements don't have one), you are in the clear.

## 2. Using the Mægler's Name, Company & Phone Without Permission

**Risk Level: MEDIUM**

This is the most significant risk area.

### Markedsføringsloven (Danish Marketing Practices Act)

- Using LokalBolig's company name and branding could be seen as creating a **false impression of endorsement or affiliation** (vildledende markedsføring, § 5-6). If the website looks like it's made *by* LokalBolig or *endorsed* by them, this is problematic.
- Using the company name and agent name as a factual reference ("This property is listed with Lasse Sundberg at LokalBolig Amager") is generally permissible — it's a statement of fact.
- **Risk increases** if you use LokalBolig's logo, color scheme, or create an impression that the site is an official LokalBolig product.

### Varemærkeloven (Trademark Law)

- "LokalBolig" is a registered brand. Using it descriptively ("listed with LokalBolig") is fine under nominative fair use. Using it in a way that suggests you *are* LokalBolig is not.

### Consent / Fuldmagt

- Publishing someone's professional contact details and directing the public to contact them creates an **implicit agency relationship** that doesn't actually exist. The mægler hasn't authorized you to act as a marketing channel.
- The mægler could argue you are creating obligations for them (e.g., fielding calls from channels they don't control, being associated with content they haven't approved).

### Recommendation

- **Inform the mægler before launching.** This single step eliminates ~90% of the risk in this category.
- Add a clear disclaimer: *"This website is created by the property owner. The property is listed for sale through LokalBolig Amager. For inquiries, please contact the listing agent."*
- Do NOT use the LokalBolig logo or brand assets without permission.
- Present the mægler info as factual contact details, not as an endorsement.

## 3. GDPR — Publishing Mægler's Name & Phone

**Risk Level: LOW**

### Analysis

- Lasse Sundberg's name, work phone number, and professional affiliation are **already publicly available** on LokalBolig's website, Boliga, Boligsiden, and other property portals.
- GDPR applies to personal data, but there is a well-established principle that **professional/business contact information** that is already publicly accessible carries a much lower expectation of privacy.
- Under GDPR Art. 6(1)(f), you can argue **legitimate interest**: the data is published to facilitate a property sale that the mægler is professionally engaged in.
- The information is used in its **intended professional context** — exactly what it was published for.

### Nuances

- This is NOT sensitive personal data (no CPR-nummer, no private address, no health info).
- The mægler could still request removal under GDPR, and you would need to assess whether your legitimate interest outweighs their objection — but this is unlikely if the info is purely professional and already public.

### Recommendation

- Only publish **professional** contact details (work phone, work email, company affiliation).
- Do NOT publish private/personal contact information.
- If the mægler asks you to remove their details, comply promptly.
- Getting consent upfront (by informing the mægler) makes this a non-issue entirely.

## 4. Property Advertising Regulations & Mandatory Disclosures

**Risk Level: MEDIUM**

### Energimærke (Energy Label)

This is the most concrete legal requirement:

- Under **Bekendtgørelse om energimærkning af bygninger**, the energy label **must be displayed in all commercial advertising** of properties for sale.
- The obligation falls on the *ejer* (owner) and "other relevant parties."
- **Penalty for non-compliance:** Fines (bøder) can be issued by Energistyrelsen.
- Even if you consider your website "private" rather than "commercial," running paid ads clearly crosses into commercial advertising territory.

### Formidlingsbekendtgørelsen (BEK nr 1230 af 21/11/2014)

This regulation specifies what information must appear in property sales material created by a mægler:
- Kontantpris
- Ejerudgifter
- Boligareal / grundareal
- Energimærke
- Sagsnummer

**However:** These requirements technically apply to the *ejendomsmægler's* materials, not to private individuals. Your website is not a salgsopstilling. That said, if your site could be construed as a commercial advertisement (especially with paid ads), you could be held to similar standards regarding truthfulness and completeness.

### Markedsføringsloven

- § 5-6: Prohibition on misleading commercial practices. If you present facts about the property (size, price, features), they must be accurate.
- As a private individual selling your own property, the Markedsføringsloven applies to you if your activities are "commercial" in nature. Running paid ads almost certainly qualifies.

### Recommendation

- **Include the energimærke** (energy rating letter) prominently on the website. This is non-negotiable.
- Ensure all factual claims (square meters, price, rooms, year built) match the official listing exactly.
- Add the listing link prominently so viewers can access the full, mægler-verified salgsopstilling.
- Consider adding: *"For complete and verified property details, see the official listing at [LokalBolig link]."*

## 5. Liability for Incorrect Information

**Risk Level: MEDIUM-HIGH** (if information is wrong)

### Who Bears Liability?

- **The mægler** is liable for the accuracy of their official salgsopstilling and marketing materials under Ejendomsmæglerloven § 24 and professional liability standards.
- **The owner** is liable for information on their own website. If you publish incorrect square meters, misleading descriptions, or doctored photos, **you bear that liability personally**.
- The mægler has **no responsibility** for content on your website that they didn't create, approve, or know about.
- A buyer who relies on incorrect information from your website could potentially claim damages from you under general Danish contract/tort law (culpa-reglen).

### The Dangerous Scenario

If your site says "92 m²" but the actual boligareal is 87 m², and a buyer sees your site first and makes decisions based on that number — you could face a claim. The mægler would rightly point out that it was your unauthorized material.

### Recommendation

- **Mirror the official listing exactly.** Do not round up, embellish, or add your own measurements.
- Link to the official listing as the authoritative source.
- Add a disclaimer: *"Information on this website is provided for convenience. The official listing at [link] is the authoritative source for all property details."*
- Use only photos from the official listing or your own photos that accurately represent the property.

## 6. Paid Advertising (Facebook/Google Ads)

**Risk Level: MEDIUM**

### Additional Considerations

- **Running paid ads transforms this from personal sharing to commercial marketing.** This triggers fuller application of Markedsføringsloven and energimærke disclosure requirements.
- **Facebook & Google ad policies:** Both platforms have specific policies for real estate ads (especially regarding discrimination, housing categories). Facebook's "Special Ad Category" for housing restricts targeting options.
- **"Annoncør" identification:** Under Danish and EU digital advertising rules, it should be clear who is behind the ad. You should be identifiable as the advertiser, not create the impression that LokalBolig is running the ad.
- **Tax implications:** Marketing expenses for property sale may or may not be deductible. This is a tax question, not a legal one, but worth noting.

### Recommendation

- Ensure ads clearly indicate you are the property owner, not the mægler.
- Include energimærke in any ad creative or landing page.
- Use Facebook's "Special Ad Category: Housing" for compliance.
- Keep records of all ad spend (relevant for any future tax or dispute considerations).

## 7. Best Practices & Risk Mitigation

### The Single Most Important Step

> **Tell the mægler.** Call Lasse Sundberg and explain what you've done. Frame it positively: "I've created a landing page to give the property extra exposure. All inquiries go directly to you. Want to take a look?"

This transforms the situation from a potential liability into a collaborative effort. Most mæglers will be pleased — you're generating free leads for them.

### Specific Actions to Take

| Action | Priority | Risk Mitigated |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Inform mægler & get verbal/written OK | **CRITICAL** | Consent, trademark, GDPR, liability |
| Add energimærke to website | **HIGH** | Regulatory fine |
| Add disclaimer (owner-created, not official) | **HIGH** | Misleading impression, liability |
| Verify all facts match official listing | **HIGH** | Liability for incorrect info |
| Remove LokalBolig logo if used | **MEDIUM** | Trademark |
| Link to official listing as authoritative source | **MEDIUM** | Liability, completeness |
| Keep paid ad records | **LOW** | Tax, dispute documentation |

### How to Frame the Conversation with the Mægler

*"Hey Lasse, I've put together a premium landing page for the apartment to give it some extra visibility — especially in expat communities and on social media. Everything routes to you as the contact. I wanted to show it to you and make sure you're comfortable with it. Here's the link: dorislessingsvej37d.com"*

**Why this works:**
- Shows initiative and investment in the sale
- Clearly positions the mægler as the beneficiary
- Asking for their comfort gives them a voice without asking for "permission"
- Most mæglers will appreciate the extra exposure
